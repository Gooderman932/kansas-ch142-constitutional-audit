# Kansas Constitutional Audit — L. 2026, ch. 142

A primary-source dossier and enforcement-record project on Kansas
**L. 2026, ch. 142 (HB 2372)** and related measures.

The thesis of this repository, stated once: **nobody in Kansas currently has a
complete, county-by-county dataset of how ch. 142 is actually being enforced.**
Building that dataset — not filing a lawsuit — is the work. It is what makes
organizational litigation possible, and it is the one asset that organizations
with money and lawyers cannot buy quickly.

## The strategy in one line

Build the record, stay out of custody, get a lawyer, sell the tooling, and move
two House votes.

## The stress-test ladder

Work rungs in order. Tier 5 is a prohibition, not a step.

| Tier | Activity | Risk | Window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Records saturation — KORA packet to a widening ring of agencies | Zero | Weeks 1–8 |
| 1 | Candor probes — disavowal letters, policy-existence probes, public comment | Zero | Weeks 2–10 |
| 2 | KORA enforcement actions against the worst non-compliers | Low, fee-bearing | Months 2–6 |
| 3 | Support existing § 5 defendants with the crosswalk and case-law table | None new | Ongoing |
| 4 | Affirmative litigation — only with plaintiff, counsel, dataset, and backing | Moderate | Months 6–18 |
| 5 | Manufactured encounters | **Do not.** See § 0.1 of the plan | — |

## Working the trackers

The trackers are plain CSV so they open anywhere and diff cleanly in git. One row
per request, incident, or probe. Keep `source_url` and `doc_sha256` populated —
chain of custody is the difference between a dataset and a pile of notes.
