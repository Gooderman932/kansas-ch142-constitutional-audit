## PART 0 — TWO THINGS TO SETTLE BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

### 0.1 Reframing "stress testing" — the version that works and the version that ends the project

You asked for a plan to systematically stress test these rights. There are two
ways to read that, and the difference decides whether this project produces
anything.

**The version that works:** stress test the *enforcement and accountability
system* — records compliance, agency candor, policy existence, budget exposure,
prosecutorial charging behavior, legislative willingness to defend the statute
in the open. Every one of these can be probed systematically, at scale, at
near-zero legal risk, and each probe produces a document.

**The version that ends the project:** deliberately placing yourself inside 25
feet of officers to provoke a citation and manufacture a test case. **Do not do
this.** Four independent reasons, any one of which is sufficient:

1. **Physical danger.** The statutory trigger is an officer's subjective
   "increased level of concern." You would be intentionally producing that state
   in an armed person. This is not an abstract risk.
2. **You have a pending criminal case.** New charges while the Cherokee County
   matter is open changes your posture in that case, your bond conditions, and
   any plea calculus. A second charge is leverage for the State in the first.
3. **Manufactured plaintiffs lose.** Courts and opposing counsel find
   provocation quickly — text messages, planning documents, prior statements, a
   folder on your computer named "test cases." The State's brief writes itself,
   and it is devastating: *this plaintiff was not chilled, he was shopping.*
   Every hour of good research in this dossier gets discounted by that one fact.
4. **It is unnecessary.** The law has been enforced since July 1. Real people
   with real injuries already exist. The Kansas Reflector piece of July 17
   appears to document one. Your job is to find them, not to become one.

The strongest challengers to a law like this are always the people who were
doing something ordinary and got arrested for it. That is a category you cannot
join on purpose without ceasing to belong to it.

### 0.2 The honest constraint

You are one person in Baxter Springs with a trades business, a software
portfolio, and a pending misdemeanor case. You are not going to personally fund
and run a statewide constitutional campaign, and any plan premised on that is a
fantasy that will burn a year.

What one well-organized person *can* do — and what almost nobody does — is become
**the evidentiary and analytical infrastructure that makes organizational
litigation possible.** Nobody in Kansas currently has a complete, county-by-county
dataset of how ch. 142 is actually being enforced. If you build it, you own the
factual record of this law. Organizations with money and lawyers will come to
you, because you will have the one thing they cannot buy quickly.

That is the strategy. Everything below serves it.

### 1.4 The county layer — where you have real leverage and almost no competition

§ 9 removed the county commission's approval authority over 287(g) agreements.
It did not remove:

- **Budget authority.** The commission funds the sheriff's office and the jail.
- **Insurance.** The commission pays the municipal pool premium that § 2 just
  inflated.
- **Liability.** The Kansas Association of Counties warned during enactment that
  § 2 could raise premiums and expose counties, partly because the act does not
  require a written agreement with a federal agency.
- **Reporting.** A commission can adopt a resolution requiring the sheriff to
  report monthly on detainer holds and § 5 citations. That is a records-generating
  mechanism you can obtain locally.
- **The public meeting itself.** Every commission has a public comment period. It
  is the cheapest podium in American government and it produces minutes, which
  are public records.

This is the highest-yield political work available to you personally, because it
is local, it is free, it requires no organization, and the fiscal argument is not
partisan. *"This law shifted an unquantified liability onto our county's
insurance pool and no one has told us what it costs"* is a question a fiscally
conservative commissioner will ask on your behalf.

### 1.5 The 2027 legislative track

**Interim study request.** The Legislative Coordinating Council — legislative
leadership from both chambers — approves interim study topics, committee
membership, and meeting days. Historically the August LCC meeting handles
additional interim topics; the LCC has a meeting noticed for August 17, 2026.
Interim topic requests are made through legislators, not by citizens directly.
Contact your House member and Senator **this week** and ask them to request an
interim study on the fiscal and civil liability exposure created by §§ 2, 3, and
12. Frame it as **fiscal oversight, not civil liberties** — that framing survives
in a legislature that just passed the law 85–38.

**Amendment targets for 2027, in order of political viability:**

1. **A press/observation exception to § 5.** Narrow, concrete, and directly
   responsive to the KPA and KAB objections that were made and ignored. This is
   the achievable ask.
2. **An objective element replacing "increased level of concern"** — e.g.,
   requiring that the person *materially impede* the responder. Note the irony to
   exploit: the State will likely propose exactly this as a saving construction in
   litigation. Make them say it in court, then hold them to it in committee.
3. **A 48-hour cap and a probable-cause requirement on § 1 detainer holds,**
   mirroring 8 C.F.R. § 287.7(d). Framed as "conform Kansas law to the federal
   regulation," this is a technical fix, not an immigration fight.
4. **A reporting mandate** — require agencies to report § 5 charges and detainer
   holds annually. The cheapest amendment to pass and the one that most improves
   every future challenge.
5. **Repeal.** Not viable. Do not lead with it, do not spend capital on it.

### 1.6 Engaging the other side honestly

There are serious people who believe this law is good policy and constitutional.
Their arguments — scene integrity at emergencies, officer safety in a period of
high-profile violence against law enforcement, the fact that § 5 requires a
warning and a knowing mens rea before liability attaches — are not pretextual,
and the Legislature heard them from the Attorney General, the Kansas Sheriffs
Association, and multiple county sheriffs.

You will not persuade the 84th and 85th House votes by treating those arguments
as bad faith. The persuasive move is narrower and it is available to you: **a
warning-plus-scienter statute that turns on the officer's own reported feelings
is unadministrable and will generate litigation the counties pay for.** That
argument is available to a legislator who supports the police and still votes for
the amendment. **Build the case for the amendment, not for the villain.**

## PART II — FINANCIAL MEANS

### 2.1 The correct mental model

Constitutional litigation in the United States is not funded by donations. It is
funded by **fee-shifting and institutional balance sheets.** Plan accordingly.

| Source | Mechanism | Realistic? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 42 U.S.C. § 1988 | Prevailing party in a § 1983 action recovers attorney fees | Yes — the primary engine. But see the trap below |
| K.S.A. 45-222 | KORA enforcement action; fees available where a denial was without a reasonable basis in fact or law | Yes — small, fast, repeatable |
| Organizational litigation budgets | ACLU of KS, press associations, national civil-liberties funders | Yes — the realistic path to a full case |
| Fiscal sponsorship | An existing 501(c)(3) accepts restricted donations for the project | Yes — far better than forming your own entity |
| Crowdfunding | GoFundMe / similar | Marginal. Funds a filing fee, not a case |
| Forming your own 501(c)(3) | Form 1023, board, governance, annual filings | **No.** Not now. Months of overhead, zero litigation output |

### 2.2 The trap you must know about

**A pro se litigant — including a pro se attorney — generally cannot recover
attorney fees under § 1988.** *Kay v. Ehrler*, 499 U.S. 432 (1991) *(verify)*.
The entire fee-shifting economy of civil rights litigation is unavailable to you
as long as you represent yourself.

Consequences, stated plainly:

- Pro se litigation is not a funding strategy. It is a cost center.
- **The single highest-return financial act in this whole plan is getting a
  lawyer to take the case.** Not to help you file — to be *counsel of record*.
  That converts a $0 recovery ceiling into a fee-shifting case.
- Your dossier is what buys that. A lawyer evaluating a contingent civil-rights
  case is estimating hours to build the record. You are handing them 200 hours of
  it.

### 2.3 KORA enforcement as a self-funding stress test

This is the mechanism most people miss, and it fits your situation exactly.

**Mechanics:** K.S.A. 45-218(d) requires an agency response within three
business days. K.S.A. 45-222 authorizes a civil action to enforce KORA, with
costs and reasonable attorney fees available where the agency's denial lacked a
reasonable basis in fact or law. *Verify the current text, the fee standard, and
any notice prerequisite before filing anything.*

**Why it is strategically superior to everything else available to you right now:**

- **Zero criminal risk.** No encounter with an officer.
- **Cheap.** District court, no expert witnesses, no discovery fights.
- **Fee-shifting** — with counsel, it can pay for itself.
- Every stonewalling agency creates a **public record of stonewalling**, which is
  itself evidence for the constitutional case (a state that will not disclose how
  it enforces a speech-restricting statute is a fact a court can weigh).
- It builds a **relationship with a Kansas lawyer** on a small matter before you
  ask about a big one.
- It generates the **enforcement dataset** regardless of outcome.

**This is your stress test. Not proximity to officers — proximity to the
records.**

### 2.4 The realistic money: your own operating businesses

You have H&H Drywall producing cash flow and PDH holding a software portfolio.
That is the funding source. Not donations.

And there is a genuine product here, not a rationalization.

The work in this dossier — multi-agency public records requests, deadline
tracking, response scoring, document hashing and chain of custody,
enforcement-incident tracking, exhibit management — is a workflow with no good
tooling. The people who need it are:

- Newsrooms and freelance journalists (KPA and KAB members are a warm channel —
  the same people you are already approaching for coalition reasons)
- Civil-liberties and immigrant-services nonprofits
- Small law firms doing civil rights and criminal defense
- Academic researchers and policy shops
- **Other states** — every state has an open-records act with the same workflow
  shape

**Product concept:** a records-request and enforcement-tracking platform.
Next.js + Supabase, which is your existing stack from `homecare-pro-next`.
Multi-tenant, role-based, with agency contact databases, statutory deadline
computation per jurisdiction, response logging, fee tracking, denial
classification, SHA-256 on ingest, and exportable evidence packets.

**Revenue paths, in order of speed to first dollar:**

1. **Data licensing.** You already ran this model with the Four-State Interior
   Finishes Cost Report. The same structure applies: a periodic **Kansas
   Enforcement Report** — §§ 5 and 1 activity by county, with methodology and
   source documents — licensed to newsrooms, policy organizations, and law firms.
   This is the fastest path to revenue and it is a byproduct of work you are doing
   anyway.
2. **SaaS subscriptions** to the tracking platform, per-seat, per-organization.
3. **Consulting** — building records programs for organizations that have the
   need and no capacity.
4. **Grant funding** — transparency and press-freedom funders fund *tooling* more
   readily than they fund litigation. This is the one grant category genuinely
   open to a for-profit or fiscally sponsored project.

**This is the actual answer to "acquire financial means."** Not a legal defense
fund. A product that monetizes the capability the constitutional work forces you
to build anyway, sold to the same constituency that shares your interest in the
underlying issue. It funds the litigation, it survives the litigation, and it does
not depend on winning.

Two cautions from prior work in this file: keep proprietary product material out
of public AI platforms; and if this becomes a real product line, sort the IP
assignment question at the entity level *before* it has value, not after.

## PART III — THE STRESS-TEST LADDER

Ordered by legal risk. **Do not skip rungs. Do not reach Tier 5.**

### TIER 0 — RECORDS SATURATION (zero risk) · Weeks 1–8

Serve the KORA packet to a widening ring of agencies. Score every response on a
fixed rubric: days to respond, statutory compliance, fee demanded, exemptions
claimed, records produced.

**Sequence:** (1) AG + 5 pilot counties → (2) 25 counties → (3) all 105 counties
+ KHP + KBI.

**Output:** the enforcement dataset. *This is the asset.*

### TIER 1 — CANDOR PROBES (zero risk) · Weeks 2–10

Disavowal letters (KORA-04) to the AG, sheriffs, and chiefs. Policy-existence
probes: does a written § 5 enforcement policy exist? Public comment at county
commission meetings. Written questions to general-election House candidates in SE
Kansas.

**Output:** written refusals to disavow — the *Susan B. Anthony List* element-3
evidence — plus a public map of who will and will not answer.

### TIER 2 — KORA ENFORCEMENT (low risk, fee-bearing) · Months 2–6

File on the worst non-compliers. Small, winnable, repeatable, fee-shifting.

**Output:** compelled production; a record of state stonewalling; a working
relationship with counsel.

### TIER 3 — SUPPORT EXISTING DEFENDANTS (no new risk to you) · Ongoing

Find everyone charged under § 5 since July 1. Offer the crosswalk and case-law
table to their counsel or public defender, free. Constitutional challenges raised
defensively inside a pending prosecution require no standing showing at all —
this is the cheapest path to a merits ruling in Kansas and it does not require you
to be the defendant.

**Output:** a merits ruling paid for by someone else's case, and a network of
defense counsel.

### TIER 4 — AFFIRMATIVE LITIGATION (moderate risk, counsel required) · Months 6–18

Only with (a) a named plaintiff with a signed declaration, (b) counsel of record,
(c) the enforcement dataset in hand, and (d) organizational backing. See
`08-plaintiff-standing.md` and `09-draft-complaint-outline.md`.

### TIER 5 — MANUFACTURED ENCOUNTERS

**Do not.** See § 0.1.

## PART V — CANDID ASSESSMENT

**Most likely outcome:** § 5 is not struck down statewide. Courts narrow it by
construction, or a single agency changes policy, or the Legislature adds a press
exception in 2027 or 2028, or nothing formal happens and the enforcement record
you built becomes the thing everyone cites when something eventually does.
Litigation of this kind rarely produces the movie ending.

**Where the real value lands:** the dataset. Nobody has it. In twelve months you
could be the person who does, and that is worth more — legally, politically, and
commercially — than a complaint filed too early by a plaintiff who cannot
establish standing.

**The biggest risk to this project is not the State. It is you.** Specifically:
filing before there is a plaintiff, going pro se into a case that needs counsel,
splitting effort onto HB 2601 before HB 2372 has produced anything, or trying to
be the test case. Each of those is a way to spend a year and generate an adverse
precedent that forecloses the next person.

**The narrowest, truest statement of the strategy:** build the record, stay out of
custody, get a lawyer, sell the tooling, and move two House votes.
