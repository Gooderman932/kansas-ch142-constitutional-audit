# Missouri Extension — Phase 1 Verification
7 Nov 2026 — verified against the Missouri Revisor, the Secretary of State,
and the agencies' own Sunshine pages. No Missouri requests drafted or sent.

## What Phase 1 verified, in one paragraph

Missouri's enacted first-responder law is § 574.207 (20 feet, Class B/A,
with a First Amendment savings clause). The 25-foot bills and the ALPR
reform bill both died in committee. The 287(g) expansion and the ALPR
guardrails both came by governor's executive order, not by statute.
The Sunshine Law gives the same 3-business-day response clock as Kansas
KORA, but with Missouri fee, withdrawal, and enforcement rules. The pilot
custodians are identified, with two name conflicts to resolve at send time.

## 1. Statutes — read from the Missouri Revisor, 7 Nov 2026

### § 574.207 — Interference with a first responder — VERIFIED, ENACTED
Source: revisor.mo.gov, OneSection 574.207, read 7 Nov 2026.
- Elements: (1) verbal warning not to approach, from a person the requester
  knows or reasonably should know is a first responder; (2) first responder
  engaged in lawful performance of a legal duty; (3) knowingly and willfully
  violates the warning and approaches **within twenty feet** with intent to
  (a) impede / interfere, (b) threaten physical harm, or (c) engage in a
  course of conduct directed at a first responder which serves no
  legitimate purpose.
- Penalty: **Class B misdemeanor first offense, Class A misdemeanor second
  or subsequent.**
- Definitions: first responder = law enforcement officer, firefighter,
  paramedic, EMT, advanced EMT.
- § 4: "This section shall have no impact on an individual's first
  amendment rights, and shall not restrict the ability to observe or record
  first responders."
- History line: (L. 2025 H.B. 225). Effective 28 Aug 2025.

Comparison to Kansas ch. 142 § 5: 20 ft vs. 25 ft; intent element includes
"no legitimate purpose" conduct; penalty is B/A, not the Class D proposed
in HB 1868; and the savings clause is express. A Missouri request must ask
for § 574.207 enforcement counts, not § 5 counts, and must ask how the
agency applies the savings clause in practice.

### § 610.023 — Sunshine Law, custodian and timing — VERIFIED
Source: revisor.mo.gov, OneSection 610.023, read 7 Nov 2026.
- § 610.023.1: each public governmental body appoints a custodian; identity
  and location made available on request.
- § 610.023.3: "Each request for access to a public record shall be acted
  upon as soon as possible, but in no event later than the end of the third
  business day following the date the request is received by the custodian."
  If access is not granted immediately, custodian must give a detailed
  explanation of the cause for further delay and the place and earliest
  time and date the record will be available. Production may exceed three
  days for reasonable cause.
- § 610.023.4: if access is denied, custodian shall provide, on request, a
  written statement of grounds citing the specific provision of law, no
  later than the end of the third business day after the request for the
  statement is received.

This is the same clock as Kansas K.S.A. 45-218(d), and the same trap:
a response is not production. The Kansas tracker rubric transfers directly.

### § 610.026 — Sunshine Law, fees — VERIFIED
Source: revisor.mo.gov, OneSection 610.026, read 7 Nov 2026.
- § 610.026.1(1): paper copies not larger than 9x14 inches, **no more than
  $0.10 per page**, duplicating time no more than the average hourly rate
  for clerical staff. Research time may be charged at actual cost, using
  employees that result in the lowest charges. Requester may ask for an
  estimate before production. Fee waiver / reduction where disclosure is
  likely to contribute significantly to public understanding and is not
  primarily in the requester's commercial interest.
- § 610.026.1(2): electronic / nonstandard media — cost of copies, staff
  time at average hourly rate, cost of medium, plus actual programming
  costs beyond the customary level if required.
- § 610.026.2: payment may be requested before fulfilling. **A request is
  considered withdrawn if fees are not remitted within 90 days, or 150 days
  if fees exceed $1,000**, with the required notice. Same 90/150-day rule
  applies to a request for clarification with no response. A substantially
  similar request within 6 months after expiry may be charged the original
  fees again. Requests pending 28 Aug 2025 were withdrawn if fees were not
  remitted by 1 Jan 2026, unless a lawsuit was filed.
- Note: § 610.026 does **not** authorize charging attorney review time as
  research time. *Gross v. Parson*, 624 S.W.3d 877 (Mo. banc).

Missouri fee language for a template: ask for an estimate before fees
exceed a stated ceiling, ask for the lowest-cost staff, and do **not**
use the Kansas $25 ceiling as if it were a Missouri rule. The 90-day
withdrawal clock is a Missouri-specific trap to calendar.

### § 610.027 — Sunshine Law, enforcement — VERIFIED
Source: revisor.mo.gov, OneSection 610.027, read 7 Nov 2026.
- Any aggrieved person, taxpayer, citizen, the Attorney General, or the
  prosecuting attorney may seek judicial enforcement. Suit in the circuit
  court for the county where the body has its principal place of business.
- Once the requester shows the body is subject to §§ 610.010–610.026 and
  held a closed meeting / record / vote, the burden of persuasion is on
  the body to demonstrate compliance.
- Knowing violation: civil penalty **up to $1,000**, plus costs and
  reasonable attorney fees at the court's discretion.
- Purposeful violation: civil penalty **up to $5,000**, and the court
  **shall** order costs and reasonable attorney fees.
- Suit within **1 year** from when the violation is ascertainable, and in
  no event later than **2 years** after the violation.
- On service of a suit, the custodian shall not transfer, alter, destroy,
  or dispose of the record sought until the court directs otherwise.

This is different from Kansas K.S.A. 45-222 (conditional fees, "not in
good faith AND without a reasonable basis"). Missouri's fee-shifting is
tied to knowing / purposeful violation, with a 1-year / 2-year suit clock.
Do not copy Kansas Tier 2 language into a Missouri escalation draft.

### § 610.029 — electronic records / third-party storage — VERIFIED
Source: revisor.mo.gov, OneSection 610.029, read 7 Nov 2026.
- A body "shall not enter into a contract for the creation or maintenance
  of a public records database if that contract impairs the ability of the
  public to inspect or copy the public records of that agency," including
  records online or in an electronic record-keeping system. A contract
  shall not allow any impediment that "as a practical matter makes it more
  difficult for the public to inspect or copy the records."
- Usable electronic format must allow, at minimum, viewing and printing;
  if the body keeps a record on a system capable of copying electronic
  documents into other electronic documents, it shall provide data in that
  electronic format if requested.
- This subsection does not apply to contracts initially entered into
  before 28 Aug 2004.

This is the Missouri hook for Flock / vendor-hosted ALPR data: a body
cannot say "Flock has it, we don't." The Kansas packet has no direct
equivalent, and a Missouri ALPR template should cite § 610.029 expressly.

### EO 26-18 — ALPR safeguards — VERIFIED, READ IN FULL
Source: sos.mo.gov, Executive Order 26-18, signed 16 Sept 2026.
- Directs, "to the greatest extent permissible by applicable law," all
  state agencies, boards, commissions, and **local law enforcement entities
  that receive state funding** and use ALPR technology to implement minimum
  standards **until a permanent legislative enactment is made**.
- § 1.1 Purging: permanent deletion of all captured license plate and
  vehicle images **within 30 days** of collection. Retention beyond 30 days
  only if directly linked to a specific, active, ongoing criminal
  investigation, or an active life-safety emergency (missing person, Amber
  Alert, Silver Alert), and strictly necessary, or to comply with a court order.
- § 1.2 Ownership: all data, metadata, and imagery remain the **sole and
  exclusive property** of the agency. Vendors are strictly prohibited from
  accessing, acquiring, selling, sharing, commercializing, or leveraging
  Missouri-generated data for any non-law-enforcement purpose.
- § 1.3 Vendor certification: when entering ALPR contracts, require the
  vendor to certify in writing that imagery will be permanently deleted on
  the 30-day schedule.
- § 2.1 Authorized purpose: ALPR accessed exclusively for administration
  of criminal justice (§ 43.500 RSMo) and bona fide lifesaving efforts.
- § 2.2 Facial recognition: **no integration** of AI facial-recognition
  capabilities or software with ALPR systems to automatically screen faces.
- § 2.3 Logging and penalties: every query must record user identity and a
  valid active case number or specific law-enforcement justification.
  Personal use, stalking, or unauthorized sharing triggers immediate
  disciplinary proceedings, criminal referral under § 576.050 RSMo where
  appropriate, and a mandatory report to the DPS Director for officer
  license revocation / discipline. Same for non-criminal-justice "hot list"
  additions.
- § 2: DPS Director shall establish strict controls and, with the Missouri
  Police Chiefs Association, Missouri Sheriffs Association, FOP, MSHP,
  Missouri Association of Prosecuting Attorneys, and others in consultation
  with the AG, develop a legislative proposal to codify: the guardrails, strict
  audit requirements, a standalone misuse-of-ALPR criminal violation, and a
  scheme for withholding state grant monies from non-compliant entities.
- § 3: DPS Director shall, **within 10 days**, make available guidance on
  best practices. That guidance was published **30 Sept 2026** — see below.
- Effective immediately, until amended, superseded, or rescinded.

Nuance to preserve: the order's own language is "to the greatest extent
permissible by applicable law," and the governor's office framed local
compliance as voluntary where possible under existing contracts and law.
A Missouri ALPR request should ask: (a) does the agency receive state
funding, (b) has it implemented the 30-day purge, (c) has it obtained the
vendor certification, (d) what is its query-log practice. Do not assert
the order binds an agency that receives no state funding.

### DPS ALPR Best Practices Guide — VERIFIED
Source: dps.mo.gov, "Automated License Plate Reader: Best Practices Guide,"
30 Sept 2026, 10 pages.
- Published as directed by EO 26-18. States it is **not a statement of
  Missouri law or legal advice**, and is a reference for agencies developing
  or evaluating an ALPR program.
- Principles: legitimate purpose, individual accountability, proportional
  access, controlled retention, auditable use.
- Routine non-evidentiary data must be deleted within 30 days, deletion
  automatic. ALPR access and searches must be tied to a legitimate purpose.
  Policies shall cover the complete lifecycle: collection, alerts, hot
  lists, historical searches, storage, access, sharing, preservation,
  retention, deletion, auditing, vendor administration.
- Agencies should conduct routine audits at least monthly, with targeted
  reviews when necessary.

A Missouri ALPR template should ask for the agency's policy **and** for
whether it has adopted the DPS guide, and for its audit logs — the guide
is not law, so do not cite it as if it were.

## 3. Bills — NOT enacted

### HB 1868 / HB 3257 (2026) — 25-foot first-responder bill — NOT ENACTED
- Establishes the offense of impeding, threatening, or harassing a first
  responder: 25 feet after an oral warning, intent to interfere / threaten
  / harass. Class D misdemeanor.
- Sponsors: Rep. Lane Roberts (R-Joplin), HB 1868; Rep. Barry Hovis
  (R-Whitewater), HB 3257. Identical bills, combined as HCS HBs 1868 & 3257.
- Last action found: **14 Apr 2026 — HCS Reported Do Pass, House Crime and
  Public Safety Committee.** Bill not on a House calendar, no further
  action found in the sources checked. Do not cite as Missouri law.

### HB 3192 (2026) — Missouri Automatic License Plate Reader Regulation Act — NOT ENACTED
- Sponsor: Rep. LaKeySha Bosley (D-St. Louis). Establishes the "Missouri
  Automatic License Plate Reader Regulation Act."
- Would limit ALPR use to criminal investigations with reasonable suspicion
  and to missing- / endangered-person cases, bar general surveillance and
  immigration enforcement, and require data destroyed within 30 days unless
  tied to an active investigation.
- Last action: **15 May 2026 — Referred to House Emerging Issues Committee.**
  LegiScan status: **Intro Sine Die** — the bill died with the session.
  Do not cite as Missouri law.

## 5. What Phase 1 changes in the Missouri plan

1. **Do not use the Kansas $25 fee ceiling in Missouri.** Use § 610.026
   language: $0.10/page, average clerical rate, lowest-cost staff, estimate
   before production, fee-waiver request where the disclosure is in the
   public interest and not primarily commercial.
2. **Calendar the 90-day withdrawal clock** (§ 610.026.2) for every Missouri
   request, from the date a fee demand or a clarification request is sent.
3. **Cite § 610.029** in every Missouri ALPR request, and cite EO 26-18
   with its "to the greatest extent permissible" limit, not as a statute.
4. **Cite § 43.032 RSMo and EO 25-04** in every Missouri 287(g) request,
   and ask for the MOA itself, the model, the trained officers, and the
   money (SCAAP, ICE reimbursement, vehicle / equipment funds).
5. **Cite § 574.207, not Kansas § 5,** in every Missouri first-responder
   request, and ask how the agency applies the § 4 First Amendment savings
   clause.
6. **Resolve the MSHP custodian name at send time** — do not hard-code
   Callahan / Drummond into a template.
7. **Joplin MO-04 is now ready to scope:** the January 2026 Flock misuse,
   the MSHP criminal investigation, and the monthly audits are a specific
   incident with a named agency and a date range. Draft it only after Matt
   approves the pilot tranche.
