## Subject

Missouri Sunshine Law request concerning automated license plate readers

## Functional definition and period

For this request, "ALPR system" means any system that automatically captures
images of vehicles or license plates and makes the resulting image, plate,
vehicle, location, date, time, alert, or derived data searchable. The term
includes, without limitation, systems supplied by Flock Safety, Axon, Genetec,
Leonardo, Motorola/Vigilant, or Rekor. The examples do not limit the functional
definition.

Unless an item states otherwise, the requested period is 1 January 2024
through the date this request is received. For Executive Order 26-18 items
the period is 16 September 2026 through the date this request is received.
I request existing records, not answers to questions or newly created analysis.

## Request

1. All contracts, quotes, bids, invoices, purchase orders, statements of work,
   subscription records, amendments, renewals, and termination records for an
   ALPR system.
2. All final or operative ALPR policies, general orders, standard operating
   procedures, directives, training materials, and prior versions, with records
   sufficient to show each adoption or effective date.
3. Records sufficient to identify every agency, organization, task force, fusion
   center, or private entity with which the agency can share or receive ALPR
   data, including the direction of access and the effective dates. An existing
   sharing-list export is preferred.
4. Records showing the configured retention setting for each ALPR tenant,
   account, database, or system used by the agency, and every change to that
   setting during the requested period.
5. Existing ALPR audit-log or search-log exports showing the date and time of
   each search, the searching user or account, the stated reason or case code,
   and the agency or database searched. Personal plate numbers and other
   protected information may be redacted where specifically authorized by law.
   Under Executive Order 26-18 § II.3, every query is to record the user's
   identity and a valid active case number or specific law-enforcement
   justification; this item seeks those logs.
6. Each currently effective MOU, agreement, task-force agreement, or
   data-sharing agreement that authorizes an external agency, including a
   federal agency, to query, receive, or contribute ALPR data.
7. Right-of-way, encroachment, utility, pole-attachment, or placement permits
   for ALPR cameras, including permits issued to vendors, businesses,
   neighborhoods, or homeowners associations.
8. Governing-body agenda materials, minutes, resolutions, staff reports, and
   presentations concerning ALPR acquisition, expansion, renewal, suspension,
   termination, policy, retention, or data sharing.
9. Records sufficient to identify the source of each hot list used by the
   agency and the operative process for adding, validating, suspending, and
   removing entries.
10. Existing records sufficient to identify stops, citations, arrests, searches,
    seizures, or case referrals originating from an ALPR alert, including the
    incident or case number, date, alert source, and disposition. This item does
    not ask the agency to create a new report.
11. Records sufficient to show the agency's compliance with Executive Order
    26-18 (16 September 2026), including: (a) whether the agency receives
    state funding and is therefore within the Order's direction; (b) whether
    non-target license plate and vehicle imagery is permanently deleted
    within 30 days of collection, and any exception applied; (c) any written
    vendor certification that imagery will be permanently deleted on the
    30-day schedule; (d) whether the agency permits integration of AI
    facial-recognition capabilities with ALPR systems; and (e) any report
    to the Director of the Department of Public Safety concerning ALPR
    misuse, including any disciplinary proceeding or criminal referral.
12. Records sufficient to show whether the agency has adopted or followed the
    Department of Public Safety ALPR Best Practices Guide (30 September
    2026), including any audit of ALPR use, and the date and result of each
    audit.
13. Records-retention schedules or destruction authorizations specifically
    governing records responsive to items 1 through 12.

## Format, segregation, and timing

Please provide records electronically in their native format when reasonably
available, preserving ordinary metadata. For logs and sharing lists, an existing
CSV or spreadsheet export is preferred.

Please treat each numbered item as severable. If any portion is withheld,
produce all reasonably segregable open material and identify, item by item,
the specific statutory provision authorizing the withholding, as required
by § 610.024, RSMo. If § 610.100 is asserted for items 5 or 10, process the
contracts, policies, settings, agreements, permits, governing-body records,
and retention schedules separately.

A public governmental body shall not enter into a contract for the creation
or maintenance of a public records database if that contract impairs the
ability of the public to inspect or copy the public records of that agency,
§ 610.029, RSMo. If responsive ALPR data is held by a vendor or in a
third-party database, please produce it through the agency's access to that
database, or identify the specific provision of law that authorizes
withholding it.

Section 610.023.3, RSMo, requires the request to be acted upon as soon as
possible and not later than the end of the third business day after receipt.
If production cannot be completed then, please provide the detailed cause
for delay and the place and earliest time and date the records will be
available.
